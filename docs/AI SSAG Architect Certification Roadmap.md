Tony's seven-rung ladder holds together technically, because each certificate does make the next one easier to justify. It does not fit £4,000, though. The cheapest credible route comes to about £4,200 ex VAT (up to about £5,050 if VAT applies to every item) with no retakes. The instructor-led route comes to about £23,600 ex VAT (about £28,300 inc VAT). One rung, SANS SEC545, costs more on its own (£6,715 ex VAT) than the whole £4,000 budget.

## TL;DR

- **Keep Tony's order, with changes to the routes.** ISO 27001 → ISO 42001 → AIGP → CISSP → AAISM → CCSP → GAIPS is sound. The hard dependencies are confirmed: ISACA says "AAISM candidates must hold an active CISM or CISSP certification", and ISC2 says "An active CISSP credential may be substituted for the entire CCSP experience requirement". Keeping the budget realistic depends on buying exam-only or eLearning routes and treating SANS SEC545 as optional. The certification to fund is GIAC GAIPS on its own (US$999), not the £6,715 SEC545 course.
- **£4,000 is only realistic at bare exam prices, ex VAT, with zero resits.** The rungs that break the budget are SEC545 (£6,715 ex VAT), any instructor-led CISSP or CCSP boot camp, and instructor-led ISO 42001 (£2,195–£3,010 ex VAT). Most rungs can be kept to roughly £450–£800 each, which is the kind of small, self-contained request a work coach can approve.
- **The revenue case is real, but it depends on proof, not on certificates.** Published UK rates are strong: vCISO work at £1,200–£2,500 per day, interim or fractional CISO work at £700–£1,650 per day, and ISO/IEC 42001 contracts at a median of about £675–£700 per day in London, although that sample is tiny. At a cautious illustrative £500 per day, each cheapest-route rung pays for itself in one to two billable days. That only happens if Tony has a pilot SME client, a public proof repository and matching LinkedIn and website profiles before the later applications.

### A3. Passing the exam versus holding the credential

- **PECB ISO/IEC 42001.** Every tier needs the exam and acceptance of the PECB Code of Ethics:
  - Provisional Implementer: no experience needed.
  - Implementer: 2 years' experience (1 in AI) and 200 hours of AI management system project work.
  - Lead Implementer: 5 years (2 in AI) and 300 hours of project work.
  - Senior Lead Implementer: 10 years (7 in AI) and 1,000 hours.
  
  Tony's 35 years and 4+ years in AI probably meet the years test for Lead Implementer. The 300 project hours on an AI management system (AIMS) are the likely gap. Until a pilot generates those hours, he would probably hold "Provisional Implementer" or "Implementer". This needs to be said openly.
- **CISSP.** Passing the exam is not enough. The candidate must document 5 years in two or more of the eight domains, be endorsed by another ISC2 member, pay the maintenance fee and agree to the Code of Ethics. Without the experience, a pass gives "Associate of ISC2", with six years to earn it. Tony's network-engineering history maps most naturally to Domain 4 (Communication and Network Security). He needs to document a second domain clearly, for example Security Architecture and Engineering or Security Operations.
- **AAISM.** This is the step where the difference bites. It needs an *active* CISSP, and an Associate of ISC2 is not a CISSP. So the CISSP endorsement must be complete before the AAISM application.
- **CCSP.** An active CISSP waives the whole experience requirement. The waiver shortens the path to certification; it does not remove the exam.
- **AIGP and GAIPS.** Passing the exam gives the credential. There is no experience endorsement.

### A4. Dependency map in plain English

1. **ISO 27001 → ISO 42001 (strong structural link).** Both standards are built on ISO's Harmonized Structure (formerly Annex SL): the same Clauses 4 to 10 and the same Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle. Leadership, risk process, document control, internal audit, management review and corrective action work the same way in both. An implementer who has built an ISMS (information security management system: the documented set of policies, risk assessments and controls that ISO 27001 certifies) reuses that skeleton. The AI-specific additions are an AI policy, AI risk and impact assessment, and the 38 Annex A AI controls. One nuance: ISO 42001 is a sibling of 27001 under a shared template, not derived from it.
2. **ISO 42001 → AIGP (complementary, not dependent).** AIGP has no prerequisite and covers governance, law and policy, including the EU AI Act, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and ISO 42001 itself. ISO 42001 gives Tony the "how to build the system" credential. AIGP gives the "what the law and policy require" credential, which is what an SME board or a legal adviser recognises.
3. **CISSP → AAISM (hard gate).** An active CISM or CISSP is required, confirmed on ISACA's official page. CISSP is the cheaper and more infrastructure-aligned of the two for Tony. (CISM needs 5 years' experience, of which 3 in security management.)
4. **CISSP → CCSP (hard waiver).** An active CISSP replaces the entire CCSP experience requirement, confirmed on ISC2's CCSP experience page. Both use one shared ISC2 maintenance fee.
5. **Everything → SEC545/GAIPS (capstone, no formal gate).** There is no prerequisite, but it is pitched at advanced practitioners. It covers agentic AI security, the Model Context Protocol (MCP, a common way of connecting AI agents to tools), RAG and vector-database pipelines, prompt injection, MLOps (the pipeline for training and deploying models) and threat modelling with the MAESTRO framework. This is the hands-on proof behind a "secure agent harness review".

## Part C3: Market and Funding Context

- **Buyer and recruiter signal.** UK adverts on IT Jobs Watch explicitly list "ISO 42001 Lead Implementer / Auditor, IAPP AIGP, CRISC, CISM, CISSP". Security GRC consulting roles ask for "ISO42001 knowledge" alongside ISO 27001. IT Jobs Watch showed 91 ISO/IEC 42001 jobs in England. ISO 42001 contract adverts in London are still very few (single figures in each six-month window), so demand is emerging, not mature.
- **Shortage evidence.** DSIT's (Department for Science, Innovation and Technology) "Cyber Security Skills in the UK Labour Market 2025" report (Ipsos and Perspective Economics, published 24 September 2025) finds:
  - the UK cyber workforce "reached 143,000 professionals in 2024";
  - the annual workforce gap is "stabilising at 3,800", down from 11,100 in an earlier year (the report's year for that figure should be checked);
  - "Nearly half of UK businesses (49%) struggle with basic cyber tasks", a problem "particularly acute in charities and small businesses";
  - "65% expect their need for AI-related skills to increase".
  
  ISC2 workforce-gap figures were not verified in this pass. Get the latest ISC2 Cybersecurity Workforce Study before submission.
- **Regulatory triggers.**
  - The EU AI Act "Digital Omnibus" (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) entered into force on 27 July 2026. It moves stand-alone high-risk obligations (Annex III) from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027, and AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) to 2 August 2028.
  - Article 50 transparency duties still applied from 2 August 2026. The watermarking grace period and a new prohibition on non-consensual intimate imagery apply from 2 December 2026.
  - For UK SMEs selling into the EU, this makes 2026–2027 a preparation window, not a cliff edge.
  - 2026 AI-agent incident statistics and current NCSC and ICO guidance were not verified in this pass. Add them from primary sources.
- **Flexible Support Fund (FSF) rules** (DWP FSF Guidance V32.0, deposited in Parliament as DEP2025-0364):
  - The FSF is "a locally managed budget", and the Service Leader is responsible for it. Awards are "discretionary, aimed at improving a claimant's job prospects".
  - Claimants in the No Work Related Requirements regime are listed as eligible. That regime is where LCWRA (limited capability for work and work-related activity) claimants normally sit.
  - Barrier awards can address costs "preventing a claimant from accepting a firm job offer or moving into self-employment".
  - Before any award, staff check "there are no previous FSF awards which may contradict or affect the current request". A sequence is therefore looked at as a whole: consistency helps, and contradiction hurts.
  - DWP training guidance flags "training requiring further consideration" and training at level 3 and 4 or equivalent. Older FSF training guidance says payment should ideally follow confirmation that the course has started, often through the Retail Trade Framework, and refuses equipment or travel for "self-sourced training".
  - **No documented cap on the number of sequential awards was found**, but none was found permitting a long chain either. Each award is judged on its merits against a finite local budget. The honest risk is that a seven-step chain totalling £4,000–£5,000+ looks large. Presenting each step as small, evidenced and self-contained is the mitigation, not a guarantee.

## Part C4: Draft Victoria-Facing Roadmap

**Hook:** Seven small, separate requests, each turning 35 years of network and security experience into a certificate UK businesses already ask for by name, and each one making the next easier to justify.

**The role in one line:** An *AI Safety, Security and Governance (SSAG) Architect* helps small businesses use AI "agents" safely. Agents are software that can take actions on its own, not just answer questions. The work is setting limits, human sign-off points and "need to do" permissions.

**Rung 0: Equipment (chair and 2TB drive).** *(Pending.)* This is the working base for everything that follows.

**Rung 1: ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Implementer.** Cost: per separate quote, confirm at point of application. *What it is:* the qualification to build an ISMS, a business's documented information-security system, to the most widely requested security standard. *Why it earns its keep:* SMEs are asked for ISO 27001 by customers and in tenders. *Link back:* uses the equipment award. *Revenue:* ISMS readiness and implementation projects; UK engagements of this kind are advertised from £10,000.

**Rung 2: ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer.** Cost: from about £459 by PECB eLearning, or £2,195–£3,010 + VAT taught (confirm at point of application). *What it is:* the same qualification for an *AI* management system. "Lead Implementer" means trained to lead the build, not just take part. *Why it earns its keep:* it is the world's first certifiable AI management standard. *Link back:* ISO 42001 uses the same clause structure as ISO 27001, so Rung 1 knowledge carries straight over. *Revenue:* AI management system set-up for SMEs; UK contract adverts sit around £675–£700 per day.

**Rung 3: IAPP AIGP (AI Governance Professional).** Cost: about US$799 (about £623), exam only (confirm at point of application). *What it is:* the leading credential on AI law and policy, including the EU AI Act. *Why it earns its keep:* it named in UK job adverts alongside ISO 42001. *Link back:* Rung 2 builds the system; Rung 3 proves Tony knows the rules it must meet. *Revenue:* AI governance readiness checks, AI policies and AI risk registers.

**Rung 4: ISC2 CISSP.** Cost: £606.69 exam, plus about £105 first annual fee (confirm at point of application). *What it is:* the best-known senior cyber-security certification. *Why it earns its keep:* it is the trust signal for senior security advice. *Link back:* it formally recognises the experience behind Rungs 1–3, and it is the required key for Rungs 5 and 6. *Revenue:* security architecture and part-time "virtual head of security" work; UK rates are published at £900–£2,500 per day.

**Rung 5: ISACA AAISM (Advanced in AI Security Management).** Cost: about US$599 plus US$50 (about £506) (confirm at point of application). *What it is:* an advanced AI-security management credential. *Why it earns its keep:* it is the closest match to the SSAG role. *Link back:* **it can only be taken with an active CISSP (Rung 4).** *Revenue:* AI-security advisory added to security-leadership retainers.

**Rung 6: ISC2 CCSP (Certified Cloud Security Professional).** Cost: £485.19 exam (confirm at point of application). *What it is:* cloud-security certification. *Why it earns its keep:* most SME AI runs in the cloud. *Link back:* **the CISSP (Rung 4) waives the whole experience requirement**, and the annual fee is already covered. *Revenue:* cloud security reviews for SME cloud and Microsoft 365 set-ups.

**Rung 7: GIAC GAIPS (AI Platform Security).** Cost: about US$999 (about £779), exam only (confirm at point of application). *What it is:* a hands-on, lab-based exam in securing AI applications and agents. *Why it earns its keep:* it is practical proof, not theory. *Link back:* it tests hands-on the controls Rungs 2–6 define on paper. *Revenue:* hands-on AI agent security assessments.

**The return:** On the cheapest routes the full ladder costs about £4,200–£5,050 (confirm at point of application). At a cautious £500 per day, each rung is recovered in one to two working days (illustrative). The whole ladder is recovered in under two working weeks, which is less than one small SME security project. The aim is sustained self-employment in a field UK employers are now naming in job adverts.
